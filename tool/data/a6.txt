Red panda|Ailurus fulgens|Red panda|Himalayan forests of Nepal, India, Bhutan, Myanmar and China|It was described as a 'panda' about 50 years before the giant panda.;;It has a 'false thumb', an extended wrist bone, for gripping bamboo.;;It spends most of its day sleeping in trees.;;It is not closely related to the giant panda but is in its own family.

Malayan tapir|Tapirus indicus|Malayan tapir|Rainforests of Southeast Asia|Its black-and-white colouring breaks up its outline in moonlit forests.;;Babies are born with stripes and spots like watermelons.;;It uses its trunk to grab leaves and fruit.;;It is the largest tapir species.
Hispaniolan hutia|Plagiodontia aedium|Hispaniolan hutia|Forests of Hispaniola|It is one of the few native land mammals left on Hispaniola.;;It is a large rodent that climbs trees.;;It is mostly active at night.;;It is threatened by introduced predators.
Philippine tarsier|Carlito syrichta|Philippine tarsier|Forests of the southern Philippines|Each of its eyes is about as big as its brain.;;It cannot move its eyes, so it rotates its head nearly 180 degrees.;;It communicates with ultrasonic calls humans can't hear.;;It is one of the smallest primates.
Sunda slow loris|Nycticebus coucang|Sunda slow loris|Forests of Southeast Asia|It is one of the very few venomous primates.;;It licks venom from glands on its arms, mixing it with saliva.;;It moves slowly and silently through trees.;;It can hold onto branches for hours with a strong grip.
Golden snub-nosed monkey|Rhinopithecus roxellana|Golden snub-nosed monkey|Mountain forests of central China|Its nose is so flat it looks almost missing.;;It lives at high altitudes and endures snowy winters.;;Its thick golden fur keeps it warm.;;Groups can number in the hundreds.
Bald uakari|Cacajao calvus|Bald uakari|Flooded forests of the Amazon|Its bright red face is a sign of good health.;;It has a short tail, unusual for New World monkeys.;;It eats seeds with strong teeth.;;It lives in flooded forests during the wet season.
Mandrill|Mandrillus sphinx|Mandrill|Rainforests of central Africa|It is the world's largest monkey.;;Dominant males have the brightest red-and-blue faces and rumps.;;Its colours become more vivid when it is excited.;;It can gather in groups called hordes of hundreds of individuals.
Gelada|Theropithecus gelada|Gelada|Highlands of Ethiopia|It is the only primate that eats mainly grass.;;It has a bright red chest patch, sometimes called the 'bleeding heart'.;;Herds of geladas can number over a thousand.;;It sleeps on cliff ledges at night.
Indri|Indri indri|Indri|Rainforests of eastern Madagascar|It is one of the largest living lemurs.;;Its haunting songs can be heard up to 2 km away.;;Its calls have a rhythm, a rare trait in mammals.;;It is critically endangered.
Verreaux's sifaka|Propithecus verreauxi|Verreaux's sifaka|Forests of southwestern Madagascar|It moves on the ground by hopping sideways on its hind legs.;;It can leap up to 10 metres between trees.;;Its name comes from its alarm call.;;It sunbathes in the morning to warm up.
Red-shanked douc|Pygathrix nemaeus|Red-shanked douc|Forests of Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia|It is nicknamed the 'costumed ape' for its colourful fur.;;It has maroon 'shorts' on its legs.;;It eats mostly leaves.;;It is endangered due to hunting.
Patagonian mara|Dolichotis patagonum|Patagonian mara|Grasslands of Argentina|It looks like a cross between a rabbit and a deer.;;It is a large rodent related to guinea pigs.;;Pairs mate for life.;;It can run up to 45 km/h.

Kirk's dik-dik|Madoqua kirkii|Kirk's dik-dik|Savannas of East Africa|It is one of the smallest antelopes.;;It is named after its alarm call.;;Its long snout helps keep it cool.;;Pairs mark their territory with dung piles.
Bongo|Tragelaphus eurycerus|Bongo (antelope)|Rainforests of Central Africa|It has bright chestnut fur with white stripes.;;Both males and females have spiral horns.;;It is one of the largest forest antelopes.;;It is shy and rarely seen.
Lesser mouse-deer|Tragulus kanchil|Lesser mouse-deer|Forests of Southeast Asia|It is one of the smallest hoofed mammals.;;Males have fang-like teeth instead of antlers.;;It looks like a cross between a mouse and a deer.;;It is a symbol of cleverness in Malay folklore.
Takin|Budorcas taxicolor|Takin|Eastern Himalayas|It is related to sheep and goats.;;It secretes an oily substance to protect against rain.;;It is the national animal of Bhutan.;;It may have inspired the myth of the Golden Fleece.
Southern pudú|Pudu puda|Southern pudú|Forests of Chile and Argentina|It is one of the smallest deer in the world.;;It stands only about 35-45 cm tall.;;It climbs fallen logs to reach leaves.;;Newborns are reddish-brown with white spots that fade as they grow.
Dugong|Dugong dugon|Dugong|Coastal waters of the Indo-Pacific|It is a sea cow related to manatees, and its closest living land relatives are elephants.;;It feeds almost entirely on seagrass, leaving feeding trails across the sea floor.;;Unlike a manatee's paddle tail, its tail is fluked like a whale's.;;Sightings by sailors may have helped inspire legends of mermaids.
Vaquita|Phocoena sinus|Vaquita|Northern Gulf of California, Mexico|It is the world's smallest porpoise.;;It is the most endangered marine mammal.;;Surveys in 2024 estimated that fewer than ten remain, mainly due to drowning in illegal gillnets.;;It has dark rings around its eyes.
Lowland streaked tenrec|Hemicentetes semispinosus|Lowland streaked tenrec|Rainforests of eastern Madagascar|It is the only mammal known to communicate by stridulation, rubbing special quills on its back together to make sound.;;The sound is mostly too high-pitched for humans to hear and helps family groups keep in touch while foraging.;;Its black-and-yellow striped spines can detach and lodge in a predator's nose or mouth.;;Despite looking like a tiny hedgehog, it belongs to a group of mammals more closely related to elephants and aardvarks.
